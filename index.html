The Universe

The universe is the totality of all existence, encompassing all space, time, matter, and energy. It spans from subatomic particles to vast galactic filaments and is approximately 13.8 billion years old, originating from a single point in an event known as the Big Bang.

The Observable Universe

The "observable universe" refers to the spherical region of space we can currently see from Earth. Because light takes time to travel and the universe is about 13.8 billion years old, the limit of what we can observe is roughly 46.5 billion light-years in any direction, giving it a total diameter of about 93 billion light-years.

Key Components

The universe is not uniformly distributed but is instead organized into complex structures.

  • Galaxies and Stars: Matter is gathered into billions of galaxies, which contain planets, dust, and stars.
  • Normal Matter: Everything we can directly see and touch makes up less than 5% of the total universe.
  • Dark Matter and Dark Energy: The vast majority of the universe is made up of unseen dark matter (roughly 27%) and dark energy (roughly 68%), which accelerates the expansion of space.

Origin and Evolution

Since the Big Bang, the universe has been continuously expanding, and observations indicate this expansion is accelerating. While scientists have a strong understanding of cosmic history, the ultimate fate of the universe remains an ongoing subject of research in astrophysics.
