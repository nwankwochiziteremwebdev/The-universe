Galaxies

A galaxy is a massive system of stars, gas, dust, and dark matter held together by gravity. Galaxies come in different shapes, such as spirals, ovals (ellipticals), or irregular blobs. Our home galaxy is called the Milky Way. It is just one of hundreds of billions of galaxies in the universe.

Types of Galaxies

  • Spiral Galaxies: These look like spinning pinwheels. They have a bright center and flat, curved arms. The Milky Way is a spiral galaxy.
  • Elliptical Galaxies: These are shaped like giant, bright ovals or eggs. They do not have arms and are mostly made of older stars.
  • Irregular Galaxies: These do not have a neat shape. They often look like messy star piles. They can form through interactions and collisions between galaxies.

Scale and Distance

Galaxies are huge! The Milky Way is about 100,000 light-years across. This means a beam of light would take about 100,000 years to travel from one side to the other. Galaxies can also have supermassive black holes at their centers.
