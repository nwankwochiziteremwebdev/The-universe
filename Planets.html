The Planets

Our solar system consists of eight planets orbiting the Sun, categorized into two main groups based on their composition. The four inner terrestrial planets are made of rock and metal, while the four outer giant planets are massive spheres primarily made of gas or ice.

The Inner Planets (Terrestrial Planets)

The inner planets are the four planets closest to the Sun. They are relatively small and have solid, rocky surfaces.

  • Mercury: The closest and smallest planet. It has almost no atmosphere to trap heat, resulting in extreme temperature changes. It takes about 88 Earth days to complete one orbit around the Sun.
  • Venus: The second planet from the Sun and the hottest planet in the solar system. It is covered by a thick atmosphere dominated by carbon dioxide, creating an intense greenhouse effect.
  • Earth: Our home planet and the only world currently known to harbor life. Earth has liquid water, a nitrogen-oxygen atmosphere, and active geological processes.
  • Mars: Known as the "Red Planet" because of iron oxide, or rust, covering its surface. Mars is cold and dusty and contains Olympus Mons, the largest known volcano in the solar system.

The Outer Planets (Giant Planets)

The outer planets are significantly larger than the inner planets. They include two gas giants and two ice giants.

  • Jupiter: The largest planet in the solar system. It is a gas giant famous for the Great Red Spot, a massive storm, and its many moons.
  • Saturn: The second-largest planet. It is famous for its spectacular rings, which are made primarily of ice and rocky material.
  • Uranus: An ice giant with an atmosphere containing methane, which gives it a blue-green appearance. Uranus rotates at a dramatic tilt, making it appear to spin on its side.
  • Neptune: The farthest recognized planet from the Sun. It is an ice giant known for extremely powerful winds and dynamic storms.

Dwarf Planets

Beyond Neptune lies the Kuiper Belt, home to many smaller icy worlds. These objects do not meet all the requirements for being classified as full planets. The five officially recognized dwarf planets are Pluto, Ceres, Haumea, Makemake, and Eris.
